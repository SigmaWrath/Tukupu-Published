### History

The Numerical Solar Calendar was standardized across [[Naurhith]] in `1.0.5.3 PRC`, by [[Elves|elven]] scholars in the great library of [[Quartzkeep]], as salience for compiling historical information following the [[Redroar Crisis]] became apparent. The spring equinox after the Redroar Crisis was chosen to start the year because of it's importance in elven culture in Naurhith. 

In present day (`2568 PRC`), the Numerical Solar Calendar is the standard for [[Naurhith]], the [[Khalsan Empire]], and many smaller entities around the world (including most arcane scholars).

### Mechanics

The structure of a date is `(Year).(Season #).(Week #).(Day #)`.

The current year is 2568. The year is sometimes referred to with the suffix PRC (<span style="color:rgb(125, 125, 125)">Post Redroar Crisis)</span>, as the year began counting from 0 beginning in the spring equinox after the winter solstice of the [[Redroar Crisis]]. The suffix PRC also serves to differentiate the date written as being from this calendar system.

Going backward from 0 PRC counts each previous year as a negative year, while the remainder of the time information stays positively signed. For example, 12 years before the date `11.2.0.0` would be the year `-1.2.0.0`. However, since the number of days between equinoxes and solstices vary slightly from year-to-year, the example just provided <span style="color:rgb(125, 125, 125)">(in keeping with solstice or equinox time markers)</span> is one of the few intuitively-defined instances of arithmetic operations with years. 

The seasons begin with the corresponding seasonal event: spring equinox, summer solstice, fall equinox, and winter solstice. The seasons begin enumerating at spring <span style="color:rgb(125, 125, 125)">(assigned the number 0)</span>, ending at winter <span style="color:rgb(125, 125, 125)">(assigned the number 3)</span>. 

There are approximately 13-14 weeks per season. Weeks are enumerated beginning at 0. There are seven days per week. Days are enumerated at beginning at 0 and ending at 6. Weeks and days both reset to 0 at the start of a new season.

 As an example, the 3rd day of the 5th week of Autumn in the year 2568 would be written as `2568.2.4.2 PRC`.
