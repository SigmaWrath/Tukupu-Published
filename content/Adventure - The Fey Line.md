### Premise
A marvel of arcane engineering, the Fey Line is an inter-dimensional "train" that takes passengers on a 36-hour journey from [[Vesperia]] (the Material Plane) to the [[Feywild]], to the realm of vibrant nature unrestrained, of chaos and emotion and magic.

Pleasantries found their way aboard the Fey Line. They quickly dream-possessed (different from Charmed) the staff (who is almost entirely elven, with a couple humans). They've been quietly stealing away depressed passengers to feed on their dreams, by using special mead (brewed in the Summer Carriage) to dream-possess them. On one of these attempts, they used a dream-possessed human passenger (Wu Ying) to try to force-feed mead to an elf (who was of a religion that didn't drink mead)—however, this failed, and the elf thought he was being attacked. A struggle ensued, and Wu Ying got hurt, which broke the possession. In the heat of the fight, they spinning-side-kicked Kameaka in the chest, sending him flying off the cliff in the Spring Carriage.

Wu Ying is now running through the Winter Carriage, repossessed after he drank more mead while hyperventilating about accidentally killing someone.

### <span style="color:rgb(203, 123, 55)">Status Quo</span>

>[!quote] It was *that* time of very early morning when you waited for your ride. The sky was slowly fading from black to purple in anticipation of dawn. The air atop the small mountain plateau was cool and crisp, and the day ahead brimmed with the promise of possibility, excitement—adventure. As such, the waiting passengers were abuzz with conversation. Elves, dwarves, humans, gnomes, halflings even—the anticipation was palpable and the atmosphere lively. The concourse of the train station was carved entirely out of the stone of the mountain, with the train tracks running beyond the full width of the mountaintop plateau. They abruptly stopped several hundred feet out, hanging over the valley below. A marvel of arcane engineering, the Fey Line is an inter-dimensional "train" that takes passengers on a 36-hour journey from Vesperia (the Material Plane) to the Feywild, to the realm of vibrant nature unrestrained, of chaos and emotion and magic. And it was on the passenger platform, waiting for this train, where all of you first met.

Have the players intro their characters: name, pronouns, description of appearance.

> [!quote] As the first rays of the morning sun broke across the horizon, the Fey Line arrived with them. It materialized from mist onto the part of the tracks that hangs over the valley below the mountain (on the right side). When it stopped in front of you, you got a good look at it for the first time. The engine and four carriages all appear to be made out of some white crystal, and each of the four carriages are covered in vines and plants. The carriage just behind the engine features leaves of red, orange and yellow hues—it represents Autumn. The one behind it has icy, bare branches, and snow piled on top: the Winter Carriage. ### <span style="color:rgb(203, 123, 55)">Inciting Incident and Act I</span>

> [!quote] Suddenly, you hear shouting in the distance. There are no other camps around, no other people... it takes you a second, but you identify where it's coming from—the top of the cliff. You might've thought before that the cliff bounded the landscape, bounded the carriage, but something (it sounds bad) is clearly *happening* up there. And then, you see *person*—knocked off, pushed off, flung off, *something* off the *edge* of the cliff. And you look on in horror, as they tumble, down, down, down, the waterfall, landing in the spray that the waterfall throws up as it hits the surface of the river. And then the lifeless corpse, washes up onto the riverbank—about two hundred feet away from you... what do you do?

> [!warning] A murderer is on the lose! 
> 
> With over 30 hours still left on the journey, catching them may be a matter of survival.

Upon investigating the body, the players find: 
- They wear very fine clothes
- They're an [[Elves|elf]] (from the pointy ears)
	- Dark skin, intricately woven hair
- DC 18 Investigation check will show that the blood on their elbow didn't come from inside the clothes—it came from outside, and it's someone else's
- They have a (closed) pouch of 44 GP on them
- They have a tattoo on their neck $\rightarrow$ History DC 10, this signifies them as a member of the [[Mikola Clan]], an agrarian community near the Waun Empire
	- Mikolans are known for being very strict about they sanctity of their minds, and are highly disciplined and place great emphasis on being rational. They also don't do drugs or ***drink alcohol***, among other things
- Perception DC 18 $\rightarrow$ they have the scent of mead on their shirt <span style="color:rgb(125, 125, 125)">{this was spilled when Wu Ying tried to force feed him}</span>
- Ticket for the Fey Line: `Name: Kamaeka; Species: Elf; Date: 2568.0.8.3 PRC; Time: Sunrise; Carriage: Winter; Camp: Bearclaw Glacier`

When they're done investigating the body, hint to the players that the next place they should investigate is the top of the cliff.

#### <span style="color:rgb(134, 93, 187)">Calling Staff</span>

> [!info] Staff Outfits
> 
> The staff wear silk finery, enchanted to very subtly shift depending on what cabin they're in. Soft, knee-high boots made from leather wrapped in vines. Filigree patterns vary for each staff member.

If the players ask about calling the staff to help:
1. At that exact moment, a parrot swoops down and drops a brochure, "Fresh off the printing press! Squawk!" into the hands of whoever asked that.
2. The brochure contains instructions on how to call the staff: Tap your ticket 3 times and ask for assistance.
3. The staff... something is wrong with them. Eyes glazed over—and did they just flash blue for a second? They say everything is going to be fine but... this isn't right
4. The staff will try to reassure the players and teleport the body with them to the engine room. This is the players' last chance to look at it in that case.

> [!info] Staff Necklaces
> 
> The staff all wear necklaces with clay beads on them representing the cars of the train (the engine and each of the four carriages). They can touch a bead and close their eyes to teleport to that cabin. If the players manage to "procure" one of these necklaces, they will be able to teleport only to any place they've already visited, and they'll see a bird-eye view representation of the cabin (not real-time).

> [!question] Carriages Shift Appearances?
> 
> Eladrin shift seasonal appearance upon a Long Rest, so the staff's appearance will stay constant despite the cabin. However, their clothes and makeup might shift between cabins.

> [!danger] If Players Fight the Staff
> 
> If the players get in a fight with the staff in order to try and smack them out of it, and single instance of damage over 3 HP will do it. Each staff member has 10 HP, however. None of them carry weapons, but if attacked while still possessed, they'll try to teleport away as quickly as possible. Upon snapping a possessed staff member out of it, they'll tell the part they don't remember anything but the *perfect* dreams they were having (they'll stress heavily how incredible those dreams were).

#### <span style="color:rgb(134, 93, 187)">The True Crime Scene - Atop the Cliff</span>

##### <span style="color:rgb(193, 145, 56)">Summiting the Mountain</span>

At this point, the players should attempt to climb the cliff. They can do so a few different ways. The waterfall makes the 800-foot tall cliff pretty slippery, so on its own, this would require a DC 18 Athletics check to pass safely. However, they can make it safely up using pitons if a majority of them pass a DC 10 Survival check. If they use [[Find Familiar]] to scout ahead, they get Advantage on that check. If any players (no matter how many) fail the Survival check, that expends one Level 1 spell slot from somebody with the [[Feather Fall]] spell prepared, and those who fell lose *something* out of their equipment. 

The players might also spot a Giant Eagle nearby (no check other than Passive Perception 12 required, but they have to suggest looking around everywhere to see what they can use). If they follow it, it lands in the forest, and with an DC 8 Animal Handling check, they can avoid scaring it away. [[Animal Friendship]] would allow them to have it fly them up the cliff.

##### <span style="color:rgb(193, 145, 56)">Investigating the Scene</span>

At the top of the cliff, the river comes from a cave, whose mouth is the only path upriver (or further forward in the train).

Upon investigating the crime scene, they find:
- A bottle of mead, shattered. It's made of blue glass, with a label on it that reads `Summer Honey` in gold. There's a bit of fresh blood on one of the glass shards of the mouth of the bottle.
	- DC 15 Perception check reveals the same blue thread that was on the outside of the carriage
- If they look for tracks, they'll find a faint trail of small blood splatters upon a DC 10 Investigation check, which they know to look for given the blood on the glass shards.

The only place for the perpetrator to have gone from here is upriver, into the cave. If the players discuss pursuing the perpetrator into the cave, a rowboat bubbles up to the surface of the river, though it starts drifting back towards the waterfall—they party members need to act immediately if they want to pursue the perpetrator.

> [!quote] To the side of cave, there's a ledge of stone that protrudes from the wall. On the ledge, is a large wooden chest, with a gold keyhole.
